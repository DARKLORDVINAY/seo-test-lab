A guide for the next small decision

Choose a guide that matches the decision in front of you. The collection moves from describing a page to connecting it, checking whether it can be discovered, and reviewing a release. Each destination has a specific purpose; reading every page is not a requirement.

A web-publishing review starts with the intended reader. Ask what they need, which page should answer that need, and whether the address and navigation make that answer easy to reach. Technical observations are useful when they clarify those choices.

Read with a question

For wording questions, begin with titles and summaries. For navigation questions, use the internal-links guide. For a missing or unexpectedly excluded page, inspect status codes, canonical URLs, crawl directives, and the sitemap together. A single signal rarely explains the entire situation.

These pages are educational examples in a demonstration project. They are not a ranking formula. A concise page can be useful, an apparently unusual directive can be intentional, and changing a label without understanding its purpose may make a site worse. Use the checklists to record observations before proposing edits.
