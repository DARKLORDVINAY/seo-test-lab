Exercise

Practice reviewing a page title

Choose one guide in this lab and write down the browser title before reading the body. Predict the question that the page will answer. Then open the page and compare your prediction with the visible heading and the first two paragraphs. Keep the original wording in your notes so that you can distinguish observation from interpretation.

Identify the subject of the page and the action it helps a reader complete. Check whether both ideas are represented in the title. If the title promises a complete method, look for the steps and limitations that would make that promise accurate. Do not add an impressive phrase merely because it sounds persuasive.

Compare two possible wordings

Draft a second version that describes the same actual content. Keep the subject specific and remove repeated project wording if it hides the useful information. Compare the two versions beside the page heading. Explain which expectation each version creates and whether the body satisfies that expectation.

Finish by choosing keep, revise, or gather more evidence. Keeping the original is a valid result when it is already clear and accurate. A proposed revision should name the reader benefit and the smallest wording change needed to provide it. This practice task produces no ranking prediction and does not require sending personal information.

Record your reasoning

Use the page-review checklist to record that you compared the title, checked a destination, and considered the page's purpose. Completing the interaction is a demonstration event, not proof that your wording would improve an actual business outcome.
